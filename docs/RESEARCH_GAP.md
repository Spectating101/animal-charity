# Research gap

Many individual animal-welfare interventions are mature or actively practiced: reunification, owner-retention support, foster-centric sheltering, adoption, community-cat programmes, source sterilization, field services, food/vet support, transport and specialist animal-assisted programmes.

The open question for this repository is therefore not whether those interventions exist. It is whether a **regional cross-organization control layer** adds measurable value by:

1. establishing why animals enter or remain in bad welfare states;
2. routing cases to existing services before creating new programmes;
3. preserving longitudinal evidence across organizations and transitions;
4. detecting recurring transition failures across a landscape;
5. proposing the smallest missing structural intervention;
6. measuring whether the population-level landscape improves after intervention.

Closest precedents should be treated as collaborators/benchmarks rather than competitors:

- HASS: community-centered sheltering, intake triage, reunification, foster, self-rehoming, community cats, ecosystem mapping;
- Shelterluv and similar systems: organization-level operational system of record/field service tooling;
- Taiwan MOA/community programmes: competent-authority policy, priority zones and population-management machinery;
- specialist organizations: qualified therapy/assistance/working-animal selection/training.

A credible contribution exists only if the control plane measurably reduces unresolved welfare time, unnecessary intake, unmanaged population recruitment, failed placement, duplicated work, or structural blind spots beyond what participating organizations achieve independently.
